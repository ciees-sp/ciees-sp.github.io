Parallel Module Architecture for SMI/SED

The SMI and SED modules use a parallel module architecture, organized around a shared instructional spine. Across both modules, learners move from understanding and recognizing, to considering factors that affect care and understanding treatment, to learning their role in collaborative, person-centered care.

This shared structure is intended to support the CPST learner personas and the range of QMHP and BHT learners who may take the modules. It is designed to help learners know where they are in the module, compare adult and youth service needs more easily, and build one transferable way of thinking across SMI and SED. Some lessons use the same core instruction when the practice principle carries across both populations. Other lessons use the same instructional frame while changing the content inside that frame. For example, both “Understanding” lessons define who qualifies, explain the criteria, and clarify the learner’s role, but include different definitions and criteria for SMI and SED. A small number of lessons are population-specific by design, such as the treatment section’s different focus on medication management for SMI and family engagement for SED.

As reviewers consider revisions, it may be helpful to distinguish between content-specific edits and structural edits. Content-specific edits may apply to one population only. Changes to the instructional spine, lesson sequence, or recurring instructional pattern may need to be considered across both modules so the parallel design remains intact and learners continue to receive an aligned experience across the series.
